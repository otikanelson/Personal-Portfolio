The Problem

Small businesses lose money, not because they're careless

Inventory management is impossibly hard for small businesses. Stock too much, and capital sits idle on shelves. Stock too little, and you lose customers to competitors. There's no middle ground when you're guessing based on last month's sales and gut instinct.

This was the problem a research student brought to me. Store managers—real people running shops—needed tools that could predict demand without requiring a data science team. Machine learning shouldn't only work for tech giants.

The Solution

Machine learning that actually serves small businesses

INSIGHTORY uses TensorFlow and LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) neural networks to analyze historical sales data and predict future demand. The app learns patterns—seasonal trends, customer behavior, product correlations—and forecasts what you'll need before you run out.

Built with React Native for mobile access, Node.js for backend processing, and TensorFlow for the ML engine. Real-time analytics, smart reorder alerts, and demand predictions—all packaged in an app small businesses can actually afford and understand.

Research Context

Built to solve a real research problem

This wasn't a personal project—it was research with a purpose. A student studying inventory optimization for small businesses needed a working prototype that real store managers could test. The goal was to prove that ML-powered inventory management could work at the local level, not just for Amazon and Walmart.

The challenge was making complex machine learning accessible. LSTM models, time-series forecasting, and neural networks had to become simple, actionable insights that a store owner could understand at a glance.

Impact & Learning
Machine learning isn't just for tech giants anymore. When powerful tools are made accessible, everyone benefits—especially small businesses that need them most.

INSIGHTORY is published on Google Play and serves as both a research tool and a functional inventory management platform. It proved that predictive analytics could be packaged in a way that small businesses—busy store managers without technical backgrounds—could actually use.
