Where it started

A learning exercise that got out of hand

Shadow of the Arcane started as a way to force myself to actually use JavaScript instead of following another tutorial. I built a small text-based RPG where the player could explore a story, make choices, and live with the consequences of those decisions.

At its core it's a story-driven RPG built around player choice — writing, atmosphere, and sound carrying the experience instead of graphics. What began as a practice project became one I genuinely wanted to keep developing.

The rebuild

I didn't want to leave it where I started

The original version was intentionally simple — it proved I could turn what I was learning into something interactive. Coming back to it later, I could see how much further I'd come as a developer. So I rebuilt it, this time with a more structured codebase, dedicated systems, improved UI, sound, and visual assets.

The project stopped being about "can I make a game?" and became "how far can I take something I originally built while learning?"

What I learned
The first version taught me how to make something work. The second taught me how to structure it, rethink decisions, and take an old idea somewhere new.

Shadow of the Arcane is one of the clearest records of my progression as a developer, because the project itself grew alongside me. Years later, it's become representative of how I approach development now: start with an idea, build it, learn from it, and don't be afraid to rebuild it once you've outgrown the first version.
